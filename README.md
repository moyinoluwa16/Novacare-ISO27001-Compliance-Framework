ISO/IEC 27001 Compliance Framework – NovaCare Health Services
This project presents a proposed ISO/IEC 27001 Information Security Management System (ISMS) compliance framework for NovaCare Health Services, a healthcare organization operating three outpatient facilities in Ghana. It identifies existing information security gaps, recommends relevant Annex A controls, and outlines a Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) improvement strategy to strengthen information security governance, manage risks, and prepare the organization for future ISO/IEC 27001 certification.

Project Overview
NovaCare Health Services is a developing healthcare company operating three outpatient facilities in Ghana. Its information environment combines on-premises and cloud-based technologies to manage patient records, appointment scheduling, payroll, supplier information, financial transactions, and other business operations.

The organization intends to establish an Information Security Management System (ISMS) aligned with ISO/IEC 27001 to improve information security governance, manage risks, and prepare for certification.

The project examines the organization's existing information security weaknesses and proposes a practical framework for addressing them.

Project objectives:
- Define a clear ISMS scope covering NovaCare's three outpatient facilities, business processes, information assets, infrastructure, and relevant third-party relationships.
- Protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) of patient, employee, supplier, and financial information.
- Establish consistent, risk-based information security management procedures across all three clinics.
- Strengthen employee accountability and information security awareness.
- Improve cloud services and supplier security governance.
- Establish the documentation, controls, and evidence required for internal assessment and future ISO/IEC 27001 certification readiness.
Key stakeholders:
- Executive Management: Provides leadership, resources, oversight, and risk acceptance.
- Clinical Staff: Generate, retrieve, and use patient and treatment records.
- Finance and Administration: Manage supplier records, payroll, administrative information, and financial transactions.
- IT/Operations: Oversee infrastructure, access management, cloud services, Electronic Health Records (EHR), and technical controls.
- Suppliers: Provide cloud, technology, and other services that may involve access to organizational information.
- Customers and Patients: Depend on reliable healthcare services and the protection of their private information.
Identified information security gaps:
- No clearly defined and approved ISMS scope.
- Outdated or incomplete information security policies.
- Inconsistent risk assessment procedures.
- No formal inventory of information assets.
- No documented Statement of Applicability (SoA).
- Inconsistent supplier security assessments.
- Insufficient employee security awareness and training.
- Inadequate documentation of security procedures and control responsibilities.

Tools and Technologies
The project focuses on information security standards, governance processes, risk management, and compliance documentation.
- ISO/IEC 27001: International standard used as the foundation for the proposed Information Security Management System.
- Information Security Management System (ISMS): Framework for managing information security risks, responsibilities, policies, and controls.
- ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A Controls: Reference for recommending appropriate information security controls.
- Statement of Applicability (SoA): Documentation identifying applicable security controls and their implementation status.
- Risk Assessment and Risk Register: Processes and documentation for identifying, evaluating, and tracking information security risks.
- Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA): Continuous improvement approach for implementing and maintaining the ISMS.
- Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA): Core information security principles guiding the protection of organizational information.
Configuration Steps
The following steps represent the proposed implementation strategy for establishing NovaCare's ISMS and improving its ISO/IEC 27001 certification readiness.

1. Define the ISMS Scope
- Identify the organizational context, interested parties, and information security requirements.
- Define the scope to cover all three outpatient clinics, relevant business processes, information assets, infrastructure, cloud services, and third-party relationships.
- Document and obtain approval for the proposed ISMS scope.
- Record and justify any exclusions.
2. Establish ISMS Objectives and Responsibilities
- Define measurable information security objectives.
- Establish information security roles and responsibilities.
- Assign accountability for information assets, risk management, security controls, incident management, and ISMS oversight.
- Obtain management commitment and the necessary resources.
3. Conduct Information Asset Inventory and Risk Assessment
- Identify and document critical information assets, systems, devices, applications, and services.
- Assign appropriate asset owners.
- Classify sensitive information, including patient, financial, employee, and supplier records.
- Establish a formal risk register and standardized risk assessment process.
- Identify threats, vulnerabilities, potential impacts, and appropriate risk treatment measures.
4. Develop and Update Information Security Policies
- Review existing information security policies and procedures.
- Update outdated or incomplete policies.
- Establish clear requirements for information handling, access control, security responsibilities, and acceptable use.
- Communicate approved policies to employees and relevant third parties.
5. Select and Implement Relevant Annex A Controls
- Identify applicable controls based on the organization's information security risks.
- Define control owners and implementation responsibilities.
- Prioritize controls addressing information security governance, asset management, access control, supplier security, employee awareness, and technical vulnerabilities.
- Document control implementation and retain supporting evidence.
6. Develop the Statement of Applicability (SoA)
- Document the selected Annex A controls.
- Record the applicability and implementation status of relevant controls.
- Document justifications for including or excluding controls.
- Maintain the SoA as part of the ISMS documentation.
7. Strengthen Access, Cloud, and Supplier Security
- Apply the principle of least privilege to employee access.
- Establish privileged access management and secure authentication procedures.
- Define security requirements for cloud service procurement, use, administration, and monitoring.
- Evaluate supplier security risks.
- Include confidentiality, security, access, and incident reporting requirements in supplier agreements.
8. Implement Employee Security Awareness and Technical Controls
- Provide security awareness training during onboarding and regularly thereafter.
- Address phishing, human error, and appropriate information handling.
- Establish procedures for vulnerability management, patching, and remediation.
- Maintain and regularly test information backups.
- Implement logging, monitoring, and incident management processes.
9. Monitor, Audit, and Review the ISMS
- Establish security metrics and monitoring activities.
- Conduct access reviews, control testing, and vulnerability assessments.
- Perform internal audits to evaluate control effectiveness and ISMS compliance.
- Conduct management reviews to assess ISMS performance and identify improvement opportunities.
10. Apply the PDCA Continuous Improvement Cycle
- Plan: Define the ISMS scope, objectives, roles, asset inventory, risk register, and risk assessment procedures.
- Do: Implement policies, selected Annex A controls, the SoA, supplier security requirements, and employee awareness training.
- Check: Monitor controls, review security metrics, conduct internal audits, and evaluate ISMS effectiveness.
- Act: Address audit findings, implement corrective actions, update risk records, and improve controls.
- Conduct a final readiness assessment before pursuing external certification.
Results and Findings
The project identified significant weaknesses in NovaCare's existing information security management practices and proposed a structured approach to addressing them.

Key findings:
- ISMS Governance: The absence of a clearly defined ISMS scope and updated security policies creates uncertainty around security responsibilities and organizational accountability.
- Risk Management: Inconsistent risk assessments limit the organization's ability to identify, evaluate, and manage information security risks systematically.
- Asset Management: The absence of a formal information asset inventory makes it difficult to identify critical information, assign ownership, and implement appropriate protection measures.
- Control Documentation: The lack of a documented Statement of Applicability makes it difficult to demonstrate how identified risks are addressed through selected security controls.
- Access Control: Inadequate definition of access requirements increases the possibility of unauthorized access to sensitive organizational information.
- Supplier and Cloud Security: Inconsistent supplier assessments and insufficient cloud security governance expose the organization to third-party information security risks.
- Employee Awareness: Infrequent security awareness training increases exposure to phishing, human error, and inappropriate information handling.
- Technical Security: Vulnerability management, secure authentication, information backups, logging, monitoring, and incident management require structured implementation and oversight.
Recommended Annex A controls:

The project identified eleven key control areas for strengthening NovaCare's information security practices:
1. Information Security Policies.
2. Information Security Roles and Responsibilities.
3. Inventory of Information and Associated Assets.
4. Classification of Information.
5. Access Control.
6. Information Security in Supplier Relationships and Information Security Within Supplier Agreements.
7. Information Security for Use of Cloud Services.
8. Information Security Awareness, Education and Training.
9. Privileged Access Rights and Secure Authentication.
10. Management of Technical Vulnerabilities and Information Backup.
11. Logging and Monitoring Activities.
Expected outcomes of the proposed framework:
- A clearly defined and documented ISMS scope covering NovaCare's three outpatient facilities.
- Improved information security governance and accountability.
- A structured information asset inventory and risk assessment process.
- Better protection of sensitive patient, employee, supplier, and financial information.
- Documented security policies, control responsibilities, and Statement of Applicability.
- Stronger cloud and supplier security governance.
- Improved employee security awareness and accountability.
- A structured internal audit and management review process.
- Continuous improvement through the PDCA cycle.
- Improved preparedness for future ISO/IEC 27001 certification.
The project concludes that NovaCare needs an integrated information security management system connecting governance, risk management, information assets, security controls, responsibilities, and continuous improvement. Implementing the proposed recommendations will provide a structured foundation for strengthening information security and progressing towards ISO/IEC 27001 certification readiness.
